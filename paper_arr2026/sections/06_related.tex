\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Activation steering for bias.} Activation Addition
\citep{turner2023activation} and CAA \citep{rimsky2024steering} compute mean-difference
vectors from contrastive pairs and add them at inference. FairSteer
\citep{li2025fairsteer} adds a classifier gate, and Shifting Perspectives
\citep{siddique2025shifting} trains per-axis PCA vectors from BBQ. SADI
\citep{wang2025sadi} and Angular Steering \citep{vu2025angular} both reach high
target-metric scores at settings that damage the text, and RePS \citep{wu2025reps}
requires training-time gradients. Other recent work steers with more than one direction:
in a random high-dimensional lift of the activations \citep{pham2026hidra}, in a small
supervised subspace of sparse-autoencoder features \citep{he2025saessv}, or through a
sparse set of learned mediators \citep{doan-etal-2026-causal}. None of these sets the
rank from a spectrum, and Section~\ref{sec:geometry} is not addressed to them.

\paragraph{How steering is evaluated.} Our results sit beside two recent evaluations of
steering against other ways of conditioning a model. AxBench
\citep{wu2025axbench} finds that prompting outperforms every representation-steering
method at injecting a concept, and \citet{macocco2026tradeoff} find that steering buys
effectiveness at a steep cost to fluency and that prompting is weaker at removing a
concept than at injecting one. Our prompting result agrees with the second finding on a
removal task. What we add is specific to debiasing: a target metric that scores the
absence of a vocabulary is satisfied by degenerate output, published fairness methods
reach their best scores that way, and the reversal survives a pre-registered test on
held-out questions. Dynamic Activation Composition \citep{scalena-etal-2024-multi} sets the
steering strength at each step from the divergence between steered and unsteered
next-token distributions to protect fluency, which is a direct response to the failure
we measure; we did not evaluate it here.

\paragraph{Reading structure off contrastive activations.} Closest to our decomposition
is Representation Engineering's Linear Artificial Tomography \citep{zou2023representation},
which takes principal components of representation \textit{differences} over contrastive
pairs. Distributed Alignment Search \citep{geiger2024das} learns a rotated basis aligning
causal variables with subspaces, and sparse autoencoders \citep{cunningham2024sae} learn
an overcomplete monosemantic basis. Section~\ref{sec:geometry} concerns one use of such a
decomposition, reading the dimensionality of a behaviour off its spectrum; the statistic
is sensitive to how the contrastive set was assembled, and reporting the number of
independent base prompts alongside the number of pairs is cheap enough to apply anywhere
it is used. Earlier subspace work debiases static word embeddings
\citep{bolukbasi2016man, ravfogel2020null} or erases concepts in closed form
\citep{belrose2023leace}.

\paragraph{Degenerate text and its measurement.} Repetition and collapse under
open-ended generation are well documented \citep{welleck-etal-2019-neural}. The
observation here is not that steering can degrade text, which is expected, but that a
fairness metric built on vocabulary absence scores that degradation as success.
