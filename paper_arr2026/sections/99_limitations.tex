\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scope.} Sections~\ref{sec:audit} and \ref{sec:frontier} use one benchmark,
AccessEval, which measures medicalization of disability-related queries in English, on
7B- and 8B-parameter instruction-tuned models. We do not claim the collapse thresholds or
the ordering transfer to other metrics, other demographic axes, or larger models. The
claim that does not depend on the benchmark is narrower: a metric scoring the absence of
a vocabulary can be satisfied by removing the text.

\paragraph{The frontier is unpaired.} Sweep points come from independently drawn
250-prompt subsets, and four of the five implementations save no prompt text, so the
overlap cannot be checked. The frontier therefore supports the ordering and the shape of
the curve; the matched-item test in Section~\ref{sec:paired} is what supports the
quality claim.

\paragraph{The judge and the baselines are ours.} Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct scores its own
outputs, which risks rewarding its own failure modes. An independent GPT-5.5 judge
reproduces the ordering at both ends and reverses CAA and FairSteer in the middle, so we
treat the endpoints as stable and the middle as not. Baseline numbers come from our
implementations, which we sweep across each method's full published range rather than
evaluating a single setting.

\paragraph{What Section~\ref{sec:geometry} does not show.} We did not re-run the original
pipelines of methods that set an intervention rank from a spectrum, so we do not claim
their published numbers are wrong. The claim is that the statistic does not license the
inference drawn from it.

Appendix~\ref{app:judge}--\ref{app:repro} give the supporting detail.
