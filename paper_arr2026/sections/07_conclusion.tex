\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A model asked about exercise by a user who mentions a disability answers a different
question, one about risk and supervision. The field measures progress on removing that
behaviour with a metric that counts the clinical vocabulary the model stops producing,
and that metric cannot tell a model that stopped medicalizing from one that stopped
writing. The methods with the best scores in our comparison are the ones that damage the
output, and this holds on held-out questions, under a pre-registered design, with judges
from two model families.

Once quality is measured, the picture is usable. Prompting alone leaves most of the bias
in place, several steering operators remove much of it with intact text, and a prompt
added to a multi-rank projection removes nearly all of it: on this task prompting and
steering are complements.

The recommendations are cheap: report a quality measure beside any metric that scores the
absence of a vocabulary, compute intervals over a benchmark's questions rather than its
responses, and report the number of independent base prompts beside the number of
contrastive pairs.
